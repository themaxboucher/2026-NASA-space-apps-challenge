## Third pass (19 Nov): every object has a model, hotspots, media and a replay

All 79 objects now have a 3D model in the tour and the atlas. The 25 that crashed (S-IVB stages, lunar module top halves, Rangers, LCROSS and its Centaur, GRAIL, LADEE, Lunar Prospector, Mars Polar Lander, Deep Space 2, Mars Climate Orbiter, MESSENGER, Deep Impact's impactor, DART) show their model as a see-through "ghost" over the crater, with hotspots on its parts.

- Ten new models were built in code (no downloads): `models-src/procedural/<key>.py`, tool and notes in `gen.py`. Rebuild with `python -I models-src/procedural/<key>.py`, then `python -I models-src/procedural/register.py`.
- New hotspot facts were checked against the NSSDCA / NASA page each one cites. Captions that the cited page did not support were rewritten to what it says (the Ranger, GRAIL, Lunar Prospector, LCROSS, Mars Polar Lander, Deep Space 2, DART and Mars Climate Orbiter ones).
- Every site now has at least one photo or clip. The last three (`apollo15-lm-ascent`, `deep-space-2`, `spitzer`) use pictures the atlas already had, copied into `public/photos/sites/`.

Every object has a replay. The six story stops keep their hand-made ones; the other 73 use a shared replay (`app/tour/scene/replays/generic.tsx`) built to the same shape: 8 or 9 lines over 33 to 58 seconds (the hand-made ones run 8 to 11 lines over about 40 seconds), the same Pause and Skip, sound, and a hand-over to the present day. The lines are the site file's own text (introduction, what one part did, a real picture's caption, the thing it is known for, key numbers, what gave out, why it was left, how it stands today). A ghost of the machine arrives the way that machine did:

| Replay | Used for | Count |

Scenes every shared replay has, from its own site file:

- What it did: on the line about one working part, that part lights up blue with a short rising chime and the view turns to it.
- A real picture: real footage if the site has a clip (14 sites; it plays in the top right corner and pauses with the replay), otherwise one of its real photos (59 sites), with the credit, while its caption is the line being read.

A scene made for that machine (all 73). Each site file names its own in `"signature"`: the hotspot it is about, whose text is the line read, and which of these plays (`app/tour/scene/replays/own.ts`):

Each machine's turning point:

- 56 have a failure moment: on the line that tells what gave out (the text of the site's "broken" hotspot), that part lights up red, the view turns to it and a warning tone sounds; then the radio rings stop and the glow dims.
- The 5 Apollo landers come down whole, and on the line that says why the bottom half stayed, the top half fires its engine and lifts off, leaving the bottom half that stands there today. For Apollo 15 and Apollo 17 the real lift-off, filmed by the rover's TV camera, plays beside it in step (the top half leaves at the same moment in both).
- The other 12 have neither: the four cabin impacts (their event is the impact), the three laser mirrors (nothing failed, they still work) and Curiosity, Perseverance, Voyager 2, New Horizons and Juno (still working, so they keep signalling to the end).

While a machine is working its glow pulses and radio rings spread from it (not for mirrors or the backshell, which had no radio). With "reduce motion" on, the motion still plays and only the pulsing and flicker stop, as in the hand-made replays.

The real sound comes from the footage itself as it plays in the replay; the effects made in code drop to 15% while it does. Everything else is an effects track made in code (`app/tour/scene/replays/sound.ts`): engine rumble, touchdown and impact thuds, airbag bounces, a rover's motor, a helicopter's rotors, a lift-off, a warning tone, data chirps that stop when the machine goes quiet, and a sound for each machine's own scene (a rumble and thumps for a quake, falling and rising notes for the laser, a shutter for each picture, a motor for digging). Nothing is downloaded and there is nothing to license; all of it follows the app's volume and mute. There is no real recording in the project for the other machines: most were never recorded, and real radio for the other Apollo sites would have to be downloaded from NASA, which was not done.

Models: the code-built models were compared with NASA's own pictures and labelled diagrams on each NSSDCA spacecraft page (Ranger, Lunar Prospector, GRAIL, LCROSS, Mars Polar Lander, Deep Space 2, Mars Climate Orbiter, DART), and each script's header says which picture. Lunar Prospector's booms were recoloured to match. The S-IVB and Centaur stages have no picture there; they follow their published dimensions.
