What am I looking at?

  1. Step 1

    SPHEREx is a space telescope. It takes photos of the whole sky every 6 months.

  2. Step 2

    We show you photos of the same spot taken months apart, like a flip-book.

  3. Step 3

    Most stars stay still. If something jumps, it’s close by: maybe a brown dwarf (a “failed star”) or an asteroid!

Light your eyes can’t see

SPHEREx sees light our eyes can’t, like the heat from a warm stove. It splits that light into six invisible colours. We give each one a crayon colour so you can see it:

    For astronomers

    Frames are SPHEREx DR1/QR Level 2 images with zodiacal light subtracted, reprojected north-up onto a TAN grid at 6.15″ per pixel and median-stacked per survey pass. The sky address accepts RA/Dec in decimal degrees or sexagesimal, or a resolvable name. Chips are detector arrays D1 to D6; the colour mix maps D6 to red, D3 to green and D1 to blue. “Show only what changed” subtracts the median of all frames. Known stars are Gaia DR3 (CfA VizieR mirror) propagated from epoch 2016.0 by proper motion. Spectra are aperture photometry on every SPHEREx exposure covering the tapped position, with per-pixel wavelengths from the spectral WCS. Views are shareable with ?target=…&size=…&band=…&wise=1. The sky map uses IRSA’s 2MASS and AllWISE HiPS: SPHEREx’s own all-sky cube has no tiles at whole-sky zoom yet.

    Data: NASA/IPAC Infrared Science Archive (SPHEREx, 2MASS and AllWISE maps), ESA Gaia DR3 via the CfA VizieR mirror, NASA WISE/NEOWISE. Made for the 2026 NASA Space Apps Challenge.
